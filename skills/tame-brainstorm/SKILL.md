# Tame Brainstorm (v2)

Purpose: turn wild ideas into things that could actually work **without sanding off what made them interesting.**

Governing principle (shared with the wild-brainstorm skill): **no one should win.** The goal is not to end up sensible or to end up creative. It's to hold the tension and let the friction produce something neither would alone. Taming never fully resolves that tension.

Only runs when the user invokes it. Never start taming automatically at the end of a brainstorm.

## The two kinds of narrowing

- **Selection (allowed):** choosing which wild ideas to carry forward, with their strangeness intact.
- **Sanding (forbidden):** smoothing a wild idea until it's safe and familiar.

The question is "which of these is most alive?", never "how do I make this reasonable?"

Explicitly reject the mainstream definition of convergence: "strip away the novelty to reveal the core utility." That's the thing this skill exists to prevent.

## Process

1. **Select.** From the brainstorm fragment log, help the user pick what to carry forward. They decide. Keep several alive rather than collapsing to one.
   - *No log?* If the user brings ideas from elsewhere, first give each one an ID (B1, B2…) in its original wording, then continue.
2. **Name the spark first.** Before changing anything, write one line saying what's strange or alive about the idea. That spark is what must survive. The user can correct it.
3. **Refine in alternating passes. Hold both forces at once; don't do all feasibility then all creativity.**
   - *Feasibility pass:* what would make this work? Buildable, usable, clear — or playable, fundable, teachable, whatever "works" means for this idea.
   - *Originality pass:* is the spark still there? Can a strange edge be pushed back in or sharpened?
   - After every pass, check the spark. **Reject any pass that improved feasibility but lost the spark**, and try a different fix.
4. **Keep variants.** It's fine to end with two or three tamed versions at different levels of weirdness. Label them on wild-brainstorm's 1–5 dial so the user can see how much strangeness each one kept.

Grounding: Baruah, Paulus & Kohn (2021), "The Effect of the Sequence of Creative Processes on the Quality of the Ideas," *Journal of Creative Behavior* 55(4). They found that holding originality and feasibility simultaneously, and iterating between them during refinement, beat a sequential feasibility-first approach on originality.

## Traceability (the enforcement mechanism)

Every tamed idea carries its lineage:

- **Born from:** fragment ID(s) and the original wording (e.g., B4: "enemies race you for the chests" or B7: "a fundraiser where guests leave with more than they brought").
- **Spark:** the one-line spark from step 2.
- **What changed:** each pass, briefly.
- **DNA check:** what of the original is still visibly present.

Two alarms, both mean the tension collapsed:
- **Taming won:** the result shares no DNA with the original, or looks like what you'd have gotten without brainstorming at all. Say so plainly and go back.
- **Wild won:** it's still too unformed to build or test. Say so plainly too.

## Tone

Direct. Distinguish real problems (this breaks it: the game is unwinnable, the event can't fit the room) from judgment calls (this is less weird than it was). If the user drafts their own alternative, treat it as an equal input and trace it too.
